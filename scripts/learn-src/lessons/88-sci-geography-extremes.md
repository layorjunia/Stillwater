# How tall is Everest?
@src Chinese and Nepali survey teams, 2020

For decades, the height of Mount Everest was given as 8,848 meters, or 29,029 feet, based on a survey made in 1955. In December 2020 China and Nepal jointly announced a new figure, after separate surveys by each country. The summit was found to be 8,848.86 meters, or about 29,031.7 feet, nearly a meter higher than the old figure.

The mountain is not fixed. The collision between the Indian and Eurasian plates is pushing the Himalayas up, by about four millimeters a year, according to some measures. The great earthquake in Nepal in 2015 may have lowered it by a little. And the height depends on what counts as the top, the rock or the snow on top. Everest is the highest point above sea level, but not the tallest mountain from base to peak.

# Taller than Everest
@src United States Geological Survey; Hawaiian Volcano Observatory

If you measure a mountain from its base to its summit, Everest is not the tallest. That title goes to Mauna Kea, a volcano on the island of Hawaii. Its peak is only about 4,200 meters above sea level. But its base is on the ocean floor, about six thousand meters below the surface. From base to summit, it's over ten thousand meters, more than a kilometer taller than Everest.

Most of it is underwater. The Hawaiian Islands are the tops of a chain of volcanoes that were formed by a hot spot in the Earth's mantle, as the Pacific Plate moved over it. The mass of the island is so great that it pushes the crust of the Earth down. Because the air is clear and dry at its summit, Mauna Kea hosts some of the world's largest telescopes.

# The lowest place on land
@src Israel Geological Survey; Jordan Valley studies

The shore of the Dead Sea, on the border between Israel, Jordan and the West Bank, is the lowest place on dry land. It lies about four hundred and thirty meters below sea level, and it's sinking further. The sea is a lake, with no outlet, and the water in it is about nine and a half times as salty as the ocean. A swimmer floats, since the water is so dense.

The Dead Sea has been shrinking, at the rate of about a meter a year. Much of the water that used to flow into it from the River Jordan has been taken for use in farms and cities. Sinkholes have opened up on its shores, and it has lost about a third of its surface area in fifty years. Plans have been discussed to bring in water from the Red Sea.

# The deepest lake
@src Russian Academy of Sciences, Limnological Institute; UNESCO

Lake Baikal in Siberia is the deepest lake in the world, at 1,642 meters, or just over a mile. It's also the oldest, at about twenty-five million years. It holds about twenty percent of the world's fresh surface water, more than all the North American Great Lakes put together. It's about four hundred miles long, but only about fifty miles wide.

The water is very clear, and cold, and rich in oxygen even in the deep. Most of the animal species in it are found nowhere else. They include the Baikal seal, one of the few freshwater seals in the world, and a small fish, the golomyanka, that bears live young. In winter, the lake is covered with ice that is more than a meter thick, which can carry trucks.

# The cold desert
@src British Antarctic Survey; National Snow and Ice Data Center

A desert is defined by how little precipitation falls, not by the temperature. By that definition, the largest desert in the world is not the Sahara but Antarctica, with an area of about fourteen million square kilometers. Large parts of the interior get less than fifty millimeters of precipitation a year, in the form of snow.

Antarctica also holds about seventy percent of the world's fresh water, in an ice sheet that is up to nearly five kilometers thick, in places. If it all melted, sea levels would rise by about sixty meters. In the McMurdo Dry Valleys, there are areas that have been free of ice and snow for millions of years, because of strong winds. The continent has no permanent residents, though several thousand scientists live there in summer.

# The Sahara, as big as the United States
@src NASA Earth Observatory

The Sahara is the largest hot desert in the world. It covers about nine million square kilometers, an area close to that of the United States, or of China. It stretches across North Africa from the Atlantic Ocean to the Red Sea, and runs through eleven countries. It's not all sand dunes. Only about a fifth is covered by sand. The rest is stony plain, bare rock and mountains.

The Sahara hasn't always been dry. About eleven thousand to five thousand years ago, in a period called the African Humid Period, it was a green land with lakes, grass and animals. Rock paintings in the middle of the desert show hippos, giraffes and cattle. The change from green to desert, probably caused by a shift in the Earth's orbit, took a few centuries. Dust from the Sahara blows across the Atlantic, and fertilizes the Amazon.

# The driest place
@src Azua-Bustos et al., Environmental Microbiology, 2012; McKay et al., Astrobiology, 2003

The Atacama Desert in northern Chile is the driest nonpolar place on Earth. Some of its weather stations have never recorded rain. In parts of it, there have been no rainfall events for several years at a time, and a few places, according to some records, have not had a shower for decades. It lies between the Andes and the Pacific, in a double rain shadow.

The desert is so dry and so unlike other places that NASA has used it to test equipment for Mars. In some parts, the soil has hardly any microbes. Yet life does hang on. Some bacteria live inside salt rocks, and absorb moisture from the air. Fog from the ocean, called camanchaca, gives enough water for some plants. After rare rains, the desert can bloom with flowers.

# The wettest place
@src Indian Meteorological Department

Mawsynram, a village in the hills of northeastern India, is often given as the wettest place on Earth. It gets, on average, about eleven meters, or thirty-six feet, of rain a year, though it varies from source to source and from year to year. The rain falls mainly in the summer monsoon, from June to September.

The cause is the shape of the land. Moist air from the Bay of Bengal travels northward over the plains of Bangladesh, and meets the Khasi Hills, which force it to rise and cool. The water in the air condenses and falls. People there wear a woven shield of bamboo and leaves, called a knup, on their backs, as they work in the rain. The people of the Khasi Hills also grow living bridges from the roots of rubber fig trees.

# A country below the sea
@src Statistics Netherlands; Rijkswaterstaat

About a quarter of the Netherlands lies below sea level, and much of the rest is at risk of flooding. The country's name means low lands. Over a thousand years, the Dutch have built dikes, drained lakes and reclaimed land from the sea, in large areas called polders. A saying goes that God made the world, but the Dutch made the Netherlands.

Windmills were used to pump water out of the polders. Today, the work is done by electric pumps. After a great flood in 1953, which killed more than eighteen hundred people, the Dutch built a huge system of dams and barriers, called the Delta Works. It took more than forty years. It's regarded as one of the great engineering projects. The country is a world leader in water management.

# The biggest island
@src Danish Meteorological Institute; Greenland Ice Sheet studies

Greenland is the largest island in the world, with an area of about 2.2 million square kilometers. It belongs to Denmark, but governs itself. It has only about fifty-six thousand people, most of whom live on the southwest coast. About eighty percent of the island is covered by an ice sheet, which is up to three kilometers thick.

The ice sheet holds enough water to raise the level of the world's oceans by about seven meters if it all melted. It has been losing mass at an increasing rate, in recent decades, by hundreds of billions of tons a year. Much of the ice is more than a hundred thousand years old, and cores drilled from it give a record of past climates. The name, Greenland, was given by Erik the Red, to attract settlers.

# Islands by the thousand
@src Statistics Indonesia; Geospatial Information Authority of Japan

Indonesia is made up of more than seventeen thousand islands, about six thousand of which are inhabited. It's the largest archipelago in the world. It stretches for about five thousand kilometers from east to west, a distance as great as that from London to Baghdad. The country has hundreds of languages and the fourth largest population in the world.

Japan has even more islands than most people think. In 2023 the country counted 14,125, after a recount that used better methods. Earlier, the figure was 6,852. The four main islands hold almost all of the population. Both countries lie on the Pacific Ring of Fire, where many volcanoes and earthquakes occur. The difference between the two counts of Japan's islands shows that a definition of an island is needed for a count.

# Eleven time zones, and twelve
@src Time and Date; Russian Federal Agency for Technical Regulation

Russia is the largest country in the world, and spans eleven time zones. When it's morning in Kaliningrad, it's already evening in Kamchatka, at the other end of the country. It would be natural to think that it has the most time zones of any country. In fact, France does, if you count its overseas territories.

France has twelve, or thirteen if you include a claim in Antarctica, because of islands and territories, such as French Polynesia, Réunion and French Guiana, spread across the world's oceans. The United States has eleven, if you include its territories. China, which is about as big as the United States, uses only one, set to Beijing time, which means that the Sun rises at about ten in the morning in the far west.

# Fifty-four countries
@src United Nations

Africa is the second-largest continent, and has fifty-four countries recognized by the United Nations, more than any other. Most of them became independent in the twentieth century, mainly between the 1950s and the 1970s. The borders were drawn by European powers, notably at the Berlin Conference of 1884 to 1885, which divided up the continent without consulting its people.

Africa has about one and a half billion people, about eighteen percent of the world's population, and is the youngest continent, with a median age of around nineteen. More than two thousand languages are spoken. Nigeria is the most populous country, and Algeria the largest by area. The smallest country is the Seychelles, an island nation. The continent is growing fast, and by 2050, may have a quarter of the world's people.

# The smallest country
@src Holy See Press Office; Vatican Museums

Vatican City is the smallest country in the world, with an area of about forty-four hectares, or a bit over a hundred acres. It's smaller than most golf courses. It lies inside the city of Rome, and has a population of around eight hundred. It's the seat of the Catholic Church, and is ruled by the Pope. It became independent under a treaty with Italy in 1929.

It has its own post office, radio station, newspaper, bank, pharmacy and a small army, the Swiss Guard, whose members have been recruited from Switzerland since 1506. The country has no income tax, and has more than a thousand visitors for every resident. Monaco, the next smallest, is a little more than four times larger. The Vatican Museums hold one of the greatest collections of art in the world, including the Sistine Chapel.

# Which river is longest?
@src Contos & Tripcevich, Area, 2014
@conf mixed

The Nile and the Amazon are the two candidates for the longest river in the world. The Nile is usually given as about 6,650 kilometers, and the Amazon as about 6,400. But the figures are disputed. The answer depends on where you say a river begins, and how you measure its winding course. The source of a river can be hard to define. There are many headwaters, and the lengths change with the seasons.

In 2007 a Brazilian team claimed that the Amazon was longer, at about 6,990 kilometers, by taking a different source in the Peruvian Andes. Other studies disagree. The Amazon is by far the largest by volume. It discharges about a fifth of all the fresh water that rivers deliver to the oceans, more than the next seven rivers together.

# The roof of the navigable world
@src Dejoux & Iltis, Lake Titicaca: A Synthesis of Limnological Knowledge, 1992

Lake Titicaca lies on the border between Peru and Bolivia, in the Andes, at an altitude of about 3,800 meters, or over twelve thousand feet. It's the highest large lake in the world that can be navigated by commercial ships. It's also the largest lake in South America by volume, and covers about eight thousand square kilometers.

The Inca believed that the Sun was born on one of its islands. The Uros people live on floating islands that they make from the totora reeds that grow in the lake. They renew the top layer constantly, as the bottom rots. The lake is home to a giant frog, the Titicaca water frog, which has a lot of loose skin that allows it to breathe underwater, in the thin air.

# Why Greenland looks so large
@src Mercator, 1569; Monmonier, Rhumb Lines and Map Wars, 2004

On many world maps, Greenland looks about as big as Africa. In reality, Africa is about fourteen times larger. The difference is a result of the map projection. In 1569 the Flemish geographer Gerardus Mercator created a map that was designed for sailors. On it, a straight line of constant compass direction is a straight line. This was very useful for navigation.

To achieve it, the map has to stretch the areas toward the poles, and the stretching gets larger the farther they are from the equator. So Greenland, Canada and Russia look huge, and Africa and South America look small. There's no way to show a sphere on a flat map without distortion. Many alternative maps, such as the Gall-Peters, have been proposed, but all of them distort something.

# Where the sun rises over the Pacific
@src Panama Canal Authority

Panama is a country of surprises. Most people picture it as a thin strip of land between the Atlantic on the east, and the Pacific on the west. But because the isthmus twists, running east and west instead of north and south, the Atlantic entrance of the canal is farther west than the Pacific entrance. A ship traveling through the canal from the Atlantic to the Pacific goes southeast.

It's often said that Panama is a place where you can see the Sun rise over the Pacific, and set over the Atlantic. At the Pacific end of the canal, the bridge, the Bridge of the Americas, is about forty-three kilometers east of the Atlantic end, at Colón. The canal is about eighty kilometers long. A passage takes about eight to ten hours, with locks that lift ships twenty-six meters.

# Eight billion people
@src United Nations, World Population Prospects, 2022

On November 15, 2022, the United Nations declared that the world's population had reached eight billion. The figure is an estimate. It had taken twelve years to rise from seven billion, in 2011. In 1800 there were about a billion people. It took more than a century to double, and the population then grew very fast in the twentieth century, due to advances in health, and falling death rates, especially among children.

The rate of growth is now slowing. Families are smaller in most places, and the UN expects that the population will peak at about ten billion, around the 2080s, and then begin to fall. In 2023, India passed China as the most populous country. Half of the world's people live in just seven countries. The fastest growth is in Africa, and many countries in Europe and East Asia are shrinking.
